Prior to this week, I associated a database with a table of data or a spreadsheet. MySQL installation on my pc opened a new world for me. After creating my first database, which showed up in "SHOW DATABASES;", I realized what Topic 1.1 meant by the database approach. In a file-based system, there are multiple files with student records, and if one is updated, the others become inconsistent. In MySQL, all data is held in one place controlled by the DBMS. This way, the centralization makes such inconsistencies impossible. However, the thought that crossed my mind after the initial discovery was who controls this centralized database server. It was then that I recalled the DBA’s responsibilities from Topic 2. Right now, the DBA is the one to establish who can connect to the database as root and who has limited privileges. If I shared the root password with anyone, the same centralization would be a single point of failure. The DBA’s job is to protect the database from unauthorized access. The benefits and drawbacks of using a DBMS are tightly interrelated. The former makes data sharing possible, while the latter imposes additional responsibilities on DBAs.
